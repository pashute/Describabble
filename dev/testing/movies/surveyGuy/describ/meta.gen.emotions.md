## Emotion Types (Non-Stick-Specific)

Emotions that appear in screenplays across genres and animation styles.

### Positive Emotions
- **Happy:** Contentment, joy, satisfaction. Mouth upward curve, eyes crinkled, body relaxed.
- **Confident:** Assured, believing in ability. Posture upright, direct gaze, steady movements.
- **Determined:** Committed, resolute. Jaw set, focused eyes, purposeful actions.
- **Protective:** Caring, guarding others. Arms gesture defensively, body between danger and protected person.
- **Amused:** Finding something funny without full laughter. Slight smile, eyes bright, body at ease.

### Negative Emotions
- **Worried:** Anxious, concerned about outcome. Eyes widened, brow furrowed, body tense.
- **Sad:** Unhappy, mourning, disappointed. Mouth downturned, eyes downcast or wet, body slumped.
- **Angry:** Frustrated, rage, irritation. Brow furrowed, mouth tight or snarling, body rigid.
- **Afraid:** Fear, terror, dread. Eyes wide, body recoiling or frozen, rapid breathing.
- **Desperate:** Hopeless, last-resort emotions. Body contorted, voice strained, actions frantic.
- **Anguished:** Deep pain, suffering. Similar to sad but more intense, body may shake.

### Neutral/Transitional Emotions
- **Thinking:** Contemplating, considering options. Eyes upward or distant, head tilted, minimal body movement.
- **Confused:** Uncertain, not understanding. Head tilted, eyes unfocused, body hesitant.
- **Skeptical:** Doubtful, questioning. Eyes narrowed, mouth slight downturn, body leaning back.
- **Resigned:** Accepting defeat or fate. Body settles, eyes close or look away, slower movements.

### Intensity Levels
- **Low:** Subtle, barely visible (slight mouth curve, minimal body movement)
- **Medium:** Clear but controlled (visible facial change, some body tension)
- **High:** Intense, obvious to viewer (exaggerated expressions, large body movements)
- **Extreme:** Maximum expression (distorted faces, dramatic body language, possible sound)

## Emotional Transitions
Emotions can shift within a scene:
- Abrupt: One emotion ends, another starts (switching on dialogue or action)
- Gradual: One emotion blends into another over time (anger softening to acceptance)
- Conflicted: Multiple emotions present simultaneously (happy but worried, relieved but anguished)
