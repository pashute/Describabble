## Bridge Types & Structures

General bridge knowledge applicable to any animation style or film.

### Bridge Categories by Structure

#### Beam/Girder Bridge
- Simple horizontal structure
- Supports on both ends or multiple supports
- Common for short spans
- Visual: Horizontal lines, support columns beneath

#### Suspension Bridge
- Cables hang from tall towers
- Deck suspended by hangers from main cables
- Used for long spans, dramatic visual appeal
- Visual: High towers, curved cables, vertical suspenders

#### Arch Bridge
- Curved structure that distributes weight
- Ornate or simple depending on era
- Visual: Curved/arched shape, supports at both ends

#### Cable-Stayed Bridge
- Cables run directly from towers to deck
- Geometric, modern appearance
- Visual: Tower with radiating cables

### Bridge Components (Generic)

**Deck/Roadway:**
- Top surface where traffic travels
- Can be solid (simple line) or open grating (multiple lines)
- Width varies by bridge type

**Rails/Fencing:**
- Safety barriers on sides
- Prevents people/vehicles from falling
- Can be simple (single rail) or complex (full fence)

**Support Structure:**
- Girders, beams, trusses beneath deck
- Transfers weight to supports
- Visible from below or alongside

**Towers/Pylons (for suspension/cable-stayed):**
- Tall vertical structures
- Support main cables
- Can be simple or ornate

**Cables (suspension/cable-stayed):**
- Main cables carry primary load
- Suspender cables attach to deck
- Cables can be smooth curves or angular

**Approaches/Ramps:**
- Sections leading to bridge from ground level
- Transitions from ground to bridge height

### Bridge Views & Perspectives

#### From Above (Bird's Eye)
- Full deck visible
- Rails/sides form rectangle or parallelogram shape
- Towers (if present) visible as points or rectangles
- Cables (if visible) appear as converging lines

#### From Across (Side View)
- Side profile of bridge
- Height of structure apparent
- Support columns visible beneath deck
- Cables form characteristic curves or angles

#### From Below (Underneath)
- Underside of deck visible
- Support structure prominent
- Cables/suspenders hang downward
- Observer appears small relative to structure

#### From One End (Perspective View)
- Bridge recedes into distance
- Deck narrows toward far end
- Rails converge toward vanishing point
- Structure and scale apparent

#### From Ground Level Looking Up
- Bridge looms overhead
- Underside dominates view
- Support columns frame view
- Creates sense of height and isolation

### Bridge Functional Aspects

**Traffic Type:**
- Vehicular (cars, trucks)
- Pedestrian (people walking)
- Mixed (vehicles and people)
- Rail (trains)

**Activity Level:**
- Busy (multiple entities at once)
- Light (few entities)
- Abandoned (no activity)

**Ambience:**
- Urban (city noise, nearby buildings)
- Rural (natural sounds, open space)
- Industrial (machinery, heavy equipment)
- Quiet (minimal sound)

**Weather/Lighting:**
- Daylight vs. night
- Clear vs. overcast
- Wet vs. dry conditions
- Seasonal cues (snow, leaves, etc.)

### Emotional Context in Bridge Scenes

Bridges in storytelling often represent:
- **Crossing:** Transition, change, journey
- **Danger:** Height, isolation, consequences
- **Connection:** Linking separate places or ideas
- **Escape:** Path away from danger
- **Confrontation:** High-stakes meeting place
- **Despair:** Final choice, last resort

Visual elements convey these meanings through scale, isolation, and viewer positioning.
